Visual Theory of Mind

Abstract
Vision-language models pass text-based false-belief probes at near-human levels
but drop by over twenty percentage points on abstract panels the moment the same
problem is conveyed visually. The consensus diagnosis is conceptual, prescribing
new belief-reasoning modules. We argue the dominant bottleneck is perceptual:
not what the model cannot reason about, but what it cannot see. We propose a five-
condition dissociation protocol that holds the reasoning task constant while varying
perceptual fidelity from visual only to text only, and quantify the result with the Per-
ceptual Contribution Ratio, the share of the visual-to-text gap a given fidelity closes.
We test this on two kinds of scenes: abstract 2D panels in the Heider–Simmel
tradition, and naturalistic 3D game scenes from MuMA-ToM and MindPower.
The evaluation covers seven VLMs and roughly 159,000 API calls. Ground-truth
scaffolds substantially close the gap in every setting, and on the abstract panels and
MuMA-ToM they match or exceed the text-only reasoning. Whereas scaffolds built
by automatic parsers, including frontier VLMs, fail. The most common error in the
visual-only condition isreality bias, which accounts for 60% of false-belief failures.
Models report where an object actually is, not where an absent agent believes it is.
This points to a perceptual front-end that fails to track which agents were present
at each moment. Under the evaluated protocol, structured visual scene-state extrac-
tion, rather than belief inference, is the dominant bottleneck for visual ToM. Project

1 Introduction
Children come to read other minds through a sequence of perceptual achievements, not a single con-
ceptual leap. Infants individuate objects behind occluders by three to five months [31], read motion as
goal-directed by six to twelve months [38, 9], and pass false-belief tasks by roughly age four [37, 5,
36]. Each capacity requires the previous as input: a goal cannot be attributed to an entity not yet indi-
viduated, and a belief cannot be modeled for an agent whose goals cannot be parsed [6, 17]. The Sally-
Anne false-belief task, the canonical assay for Theory of Mind, sits at the top of this chain [5, 25].
When this task was adapted for large language models, frontier systems passed text-based probes
at near-human levels [16, 33]. The moment the same problem is conveyed visually, performance
collapses by twenty or more percentage points across every VLM tested [ 18, 8], with even larger
gaps on naturalistic multi-agent video [29, 14, 39]. The consensus diagnosis is conceptual: VLMs
are taken to lack the machinery belief attribution requires, prescribing explicit social-reasoning
modules atop existing visual encoders [35, 27, 28].
We argue that this diagnosis is incomplete. The bottleneck is not what the model cannot reason
about; it is what it cannot see. If a VLM solves a false-belief problem at 94.5% in text but only
73.9% in pixels, the deficit lies in the perceptual pipeline that converts pixels into propositions, not
in the reasoning that operates on them. We call this theperceptual bottleneck hypothesis: visual
ToM failures reflect breakdowns in structured perceptual-state extraction (entity identity, position,
presence, absence across panels or frames), not in downstream belief reasoning. We useperception
operationally for this structured scene-state extraction, including cross-frame correspondence
and event detection, andreasoningfor the mapping from scene states and access facts to belief
attributions and answers (§3). The hypothesis is falsifiable. If the bottleneck is perceptual, giving
the model the ground-truth scene description should bring performance back toward the text-only
reasoning. If the bottleneck is conceptual, no scaffold will help, however accurate it is.
To test this, we propose a five-condition dissociation protocol that holds the reasoning task constant
while varying perceptual fidelity from visual only to text only with three intermediate scaffolds.
We instantiate it on two stimulus regimes following prior conventions:abstract 2D panelsin the
Heider-Simmel tradition [11], organized along the developmental cascade, andnaturalistic 3D game
scenesfrom MuMA-ToM [ 29] and MindPower [39]. We quantify scaffold benefit with thePerceptual
Contribution Ratio(PCR), a dose-response metric taking value 1 when a scaffold fully recovers the
text-only ceiling and0when it does not help.
The signature reproduces across both regimes and seven VLMs spanning three capability tiers.
Ground-truth scaffolds substantially close the visual-to-text gap on every regime, matching or
exceeding the text-only ceiling on abstract panels and MuMA-ToM and recovering 59% of it on
MindPower. Automatic parsers, including frontier VLMs, fail. Stripping the explicit visibility
and absence statements from the ground-truth scaffold by pattern matching retains most of the
gain, ruling out that scaffolds simply hand the model the reasoning predicate. A finer content
ladder attributes the largest aggregate increment to cross-frame event structure, and length-matched
corrupted scaffolds recover less than half of the correct scaffold’s gain on aggregate, arguing against
text volume as the explanation. The most common error in the visual-only condition isreality bias,
which accounts for 60% of false-belief failures. Models report where an object actually is, not where
an absent agent believes it is. This points to a perceptual front-end that fails to track which agents
were present at each moment. When the front-end fails to recover the state of each entity in the scene,
the downstream reasoning cannot make up for it. Under the evaluated protocol, structured visual
scene-state extraction, rather than belief inference, is the dominant bottleneck for visual ToM.
Contributions:
• A five-condition dissociation protocol isolating perceptual from conceptual contributions to visual
ToM failures, applied uniformly across abstract 2D and naturalistic 3D regimes.
• The Perceptual Contribution Ratio: a dose-response metric tracing how much of the visual-to-text
gap a scaffold of given fidelity closes, with negative values flagging misleading scaffolds and
values above1indicating the combination exceeds the text-only ceiling.
• Cross-regime evidence, across seven VLMs and roughly 159,000 API calls, that the visual ToM
deficit is predominantly perceptual rather than conceptual, supported by a scaffold-content ladder,
length-matched corruption controls, and the reality-bias error taxonomy.

2 Related work
Theory of Mind in language models:Whether LLMs possess Theory of Mind remains contested.
Kosinski [16] reported GPT-4 passes false-belief tasks at six-year-old levels; Ullman [35] showed that
trivial paraphrases cause catastrophic failures. Strachanet al. [ 33] found above-chance belief tracking
with systematic biases absent in humans; Gandhiet al. [ 7] documented uneven performance across
ToM dimensions; Shapiraet al. [28] introduced adversarial controls that degrade LLM ToM; Huet
al. [13] argue existing evaluations conflate linguistic competence with mentalizing; Kimet al. [15]
stress-tested ToM in information-asymmetric conversations. These studies are text-only. In the visual
domain, Jinet al. [ 14] introduced MMToM-QA; Shiet al. [ 29] extended this with MuMA-ToM;
Chenet al. [ 8] showed VLMs fail at visual perspective-taking despite intent recognition; Zhanget
al. [39] documented a substantial human-VLM gap with MindPower. None measures whether the
visual deficit is perceptual or conceptual in origin. Our protocol forces that distinction by holding
the reasoning task constant and varying only the fidelity of perceptual input, applied uniformly across
abstract 2D panels and naturalistic 3D game scenes.
Cognitive benchmarks for VLMs:CogDevelop2K [ 18] evaluated 46 multimodal LLMs on 12
cognitive sub-concepts organized by developmental milestones, finding models outperform on
late-developing abstract reasoning relative to early-developing perceptual tasks. Tonget al. [34] traced
systematic visual shortcomings to CLIP-based encoders; MotionBench [ 12] reported substantial
gaps in fine-grained video motion understanding. These benchmarks score VLMs but treat the
resulting accuracy as a single number; perceptual and inferential contributions remain entangled. Our
scaffolding intervention dissociates the two and assigns the deficit to perception rather than reasoning.
Developmental foundations:Spelke’s core knowledge theory [ 31, 32] posits domain-specific
representational systems with a cascaded developmental trajectory: object individuation through
spatiotemporal continuity [31] is a prerequisite for goal attribution [38, 9], which is a prerequisite
for belief modeling [37, 5, 6]. Leslie [ 17] formalized this as a metarepresentational requirement;
Onishi and Baillargeon [22] and Baillargeonet al. [ 4] demonstrated implicit false-belief sensitivity
in infancy; Wellmanet al. [36] meta-analyzed 178 studies confirming robust developmental change.
Apperly and Butterfill [2] proposed dual-systems distinguishing efficient inflexible belief tracking
from flexible effortful mentalizing; Shuet al. [ 30] operationalized core psychological reasoning in
AGENT. This literature establishes the cascade as a property of human development; it does not
test whether VLMs are subject to a parallel dependency. Our protocol provides that test, and the
dose-response signature places the bottleneck in the front-end of the cascade.
Model-based ToM and structured intermediates:Several approaches assume access to structured
state representations as input. ToMNet [26] trains a meta-learner over agent trajectories represented
as state-action sequences; BIP-ALM [14] combines Bayesian inverse planning with structured scene
representations to achieve strong belief inference. These systems take structured intermediates as
given and evaluate the reasoning that follows. Our protocol takes the inverse view: it holds the
inferential pathway constant and varies the fidelity of the structured intermediate that visual encoders
attempt to produce, isolating the cost of extracting that intermediate from pixels.
3 Benchmarking protocol
We treat visual ToM as two pipelines in series: a perceptual front-end converting pixels into
propositions, and an inferential back-end mapping them and a question to an answer. A single
accuracy figure under visual input cannot tell us which is failing. Our protocol forces them apart
by holding the inferential task constant while parametrically varying perceptual fidelity, from no
scaffold to a complete ground-truth scene description. It applies identically to abstract 2D panels
and naturalistic 3D game scenes.
Operational definition:Perception is structured scene-state extraction: entity identity, position, and
presence or absence across panels or frames, including the cross-frame correspondence and event
detection that link them. Like object individuation and tracking through occlusion [31, 4], these com-
petences carry no mental-state content. Reasoning maps such scene states and access facts to belief
attributions and answers. Cross-frame event linking is an intermediate representation; we assign it to
perception by this definition rather than assume the boundary, and no scaffold belowBf states a belief.

3.1 The five-condition dissociation
Each probe is evaluated under five conditions, sampling the visual-to-text fidelity continuum. A
and C anchor the endpoints; the three B conditions sit between, each adding a textual scaffold of
increasing fidelity to the image.
A Visual only:Multi-panel image (abstract) or sampled video frames (naturalistic) with the
question; no scene description (verbatim prompts in §H). Measures how well the model’s own
front-end extracts the scene state the question requires.
Ba Image with automatic-parser scaffold:Visual input plus a scaffold from a parser with no
ground-truth access. Abstract: a hand-engineered pixel pipeline using flood-fill blob detection, color
matching, and inter-panel differencing (§A.2). Naturalistic: a frontier VLM (GPT-4o; additionally
Claude Sonnet 4 on MindPower), prompted to produce panel-by-panel entity narrations with no ToM
interpretation. Both are imperfect by construction; Ba asks whether a realistic front-end recovers
the scene structure belief attribution requires.
Bp Image with perceptual-grade scaffold:Visual input plus a scaffold correct by construction but
deliberately omitting visibility and absence inference: it names entities, positions, and observable
events, may state that an agent witnesses an event, but never states that one agent did not observe
what another did. This isolates how much scaffold benefit comes from perceptual content rather than
visibility cues. Abstract: a panel-by-panel entity narration generated programmatically from the scene
generator’s state. MindPower: the dataset’s scene description (script-derived for 427 of 456 Virtual-
Home scenarios, human-written otherwise), which contains observable scene state but not the belief,
desire, intention, decision, or action layers used in scoring, and occasionally contains state-comparison
phrasing (e.g. “the box is closed again”). Undefined for MuMA-ToM, whose annotations yield a
single scaffold; it matches no visibility pattern (below), so thereB f is close to perceptual-grade.
Bf Image with full ground-truth scaffold:Visual input plus the most complete reference scaffold
per regime. Abstract: Bp plus explicit visibility and absence statements (“agent X was not in the
scene when the object was moved”); for the second-order template, the scaffold also states the
resulting first-order belief. MuMA-ToM: ground-truth entity identities, locations, and actions; none
of the 900 logged prompts matches the visibility patterns of §G.2 (§G.9). MindPower: the full human
reference chain across all six layers, shared across all scored layers of a scenario; on this regime, Bf
functions as a full-information oracle upper bound rather than a leakage-controlled perceptual ceiling.

Why these five conditions:Each transition adds one interpretable increment of fidelity while
question and image are held fixed: A →B a adds what a realistic automatic front-end produces;
Ba →B p makes the content correct while withholding visibility and absence; Bp →B f adds
visibility and absence; Bf →C removes the image. The endpoints are the settings prior work
measures; the intermediate rungs separate front-end quality (Ba vs. Bp) from epistemic content (Bp
vs. Bf ) and from the image ( Bf vs. C). Accuracy therefore traces a dose-response along a single
axis, the fidelity of perceptual input reaching the inferential pathway, which PCR (§3.3) reads rung
by rung; §4 refines the ladder belowB p.
3.2 Stimulus regimes
The two regimes differ on every perceptual dimension.Abstract 2D panelspresent ToM structure
in stripped-down form: multi-panel images of geometric shapes in the Heider-Simmel tradition [11],
with zero embedded text.Naturalistic 3D game scenesmove to multi-agent household activity
rendered in 3D game environments, drawing on MuMA-ToM [29] and MindPower [39]. The two
stress-test the perceptual-bottleneck account from opposite directions: abstract asks whether the
bottleneck appears even when stimuli are deliberately easy to parse; naturalistic asks whether the
same dose-response reproduces under realistic clutter, occlusion, and multi-frame dynamics.
Abstract 2D panels:Following the developmental cascade in human cognitive develop-
ment [31, 6, 36], the regime spans three stages, each requiring the previous as computational input
(full diagram in §C). Stage 1 (Object Tracking, 6 templates, 300 probes) tests spatiotemporal
individuation: occlusion continuity, trajectory-violation detection, count maintenance, permanence,
color-change [31]. Stage 2 (Goal Attribution, 4 templates, 200 probes) tests inference of goals from
motion, corresponding to 6–12 month infant capacities [38, 9]: target inference, detour reasoning,
non-goal discrimination, efficient action prediction. Stage 3 (False Belief, 10 templates, 500 probes)
tests the canonical ToM capacity [ 37, 5, 36]: Sally-Anne search and belief reports, reality-belief
dissociation, knowledge access, unexpected contents, two-room transfers, perspective divergence, and
second-order belief. Each template gets 50 seeded randomized variants for 1,000 probes total (gener-
ation and seed formula in §A.1). We follow construction conventions of prior work and do not claim
the panel set as a contribution; representative probes in Figure 2; full template descriptions in §C.

3.3 The Perceptual Contribution Ratio
The five conditions yield five accuracy numbers per model and benchmark. We collapse them to
a single ratio: of the visual-to-text deficit Acc(C)−Acc(A) , what fraction does a scaffold of given
fidelity recover?
A VLM processes a stimulus I through perceptual extraction π, producing internal propositions,
followed by reasoning ρ mapping propositions and a question q to an answer: fvis(I,q) =ρ(π(I),q) .
In text-only mode, ftxt(T, q) =ρ(τ(T), q) , where τ encodes a narration T into the same
propositional space. A scaffold sx at fidelity x augments visual input via token-level fusion:
fscaffold(I,s x,q) =ρ(π(I)⊕τ(s x),q). Treating ρ as shared across modalities is a simplification; the
protocol tests its functional consequence rather than its mechanistic identity.

3.4 Models and evaluation
Model lineup:We evaluate seven VLMs across three tiers reflecting parameter count and provider
positioning.Frontier(top-line commercial, >100B): GPT-4o (Nov. 2024) [23], Gemini 2.5 Pro
Preview [10], Claude Sonnet 4 [1].Mid-scale(17B–72B): Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct [ 3], Llama
4 Maverick [20].Small-scale(sub-10B or mini): GPT-4.1-mini [24], Ministral 3 8B [21]. All seven
are evaluated on both regimes. Models are accessed through the OpenRouter API with deterministic
decoding (temperature 0, max 200 output tokens, single-turn); request parameters and retry logic
in §A.3, exact model identifiers in §F, and verbatim prompts for every condition in §H.
Answer grading:Responses are normalized (lowercased, whitespace-collapsed) before deterministic
grading with no partial credit. For yes/no, the response must begin with the expected token; for numer-
ical, the expected digit must appear in the first four characters; for directional, the response must con-
tain any expected alias, including container color names, where unambiguous; for the reality-versus-
belief template, the response is split at “belief” with the reality answer before and belief answer after.
Identical across all conditions and models; alias lists and split-answer algorithms in §A.4. MuMA-
ToM uses the multiple-choice grading from [29]; MindPower uses the per-layer scoring from [39].
Statistical methodology:Confidence intervals are computed by probe-level bootstrap with 10,000
iterations, resampling probe indices with replacement and reporting the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles.
PCR CIs use the same bootstrap, recomputing the ratio per iteration. Pairwise condition comparisons
use McNemar’s test on matched (model, probe) pairs with Bonferroni correction across the 12
protocol comparisons (αBonf = 0.0042); for abstract Stage 3, this yields n= 7×500 = 3,500paired
observations per comparison. The ladder and corruption controls (§4) use the same test and threshold
against the same A. Full detail in §D.
Scale:The protocol totals roughly 159,000 API calls: ∼111,000 on the abstract regime (including
the content ladder, corruption controls, and ablations on Bvlm, Bvis−stripped, chain-of-thought, and
self-scaffold reported in §G) and ∼48,000 on the naturalistic regime. Parser-failure responses are
excluded from totals.

CI [1.09, 1.30]). The Bf -plus-image condition matches or exceeds the text-only ceiling for every
tier (Table 2a): frontier 1.22, mid-scale 1.01, small-scale 1.26. Naturalistic MuMA-ToM shows
the same pattern at a larger magnitude: aggregate PCRf = 1.44, frontier 1.81, mid-scale 1.57,
small-scale 0.66 (Table 2b). These scaffolds match none of the visibility patterns of §G.2 (§G.9),
so content without explicit visibility statements closes the gap. Because Bf and C also differ in their
text on abstract panels, we do not interpret PCR>1 further. On MindPower, the picture changes
quantitatively but not qualitatively: Bf recovers 59% of a 13.9 pp visual-to-text gap, with mid-scale
strongest at PCRf = 0.86. On this regime, Bf is an oracle full-information scaffold (§3.1); the
leakage-controlled Bp recovers 39% of the gap on the frontier and 42% on mid-scale (Table 2c).
Across all three regimes, every PCRf is positive and statistically distinct from zero (Table 2 CIs;
McNemarp Bonf <10 −4 on the binary-scored regimes).
Automatic parsers fail across regimes:Where ground-truth scaffolds rescue performance,
automatic-parser scaffolds do not (Table 2, PCRa column). On abstract 2D Stage 3, the aggregate
PCRa is 0.09, with the mid-scale tier at −1.10: the parser actively misleads. The small-scale tier is
the exception (PCRa = 0.82). The hand-engineered abstract parser correctly recovers entity identities
and positions on 99.4% of probes (§B.2), so the failure is not at low-level extraction. The deficit
is that parser output, even when correct on entity-level facts, lacks the visibility and presence-absence
structure false-belief requires. On MindPower we ran two independent parsers: GPT-4o-as-parser
yields aggregate PCRa =−0.06 across tiers and Claude-as-parser yields −0.08 (scenario-weighted
average of environment PCRs; Table 5). Failure consistency across two frontier parsers, in addition
to the hand-engineered parser on abstract panels, is the diagnostic: the bottleneck is not bridged
by current visual encoders in zero-shot scene-describer roles.
The dose-response is monotonic in scaffold fidelity:Where all three scaffold conditions are defined
(abstract 2D Stage 3 and MindPower), accuracy increases monotonically from A through Ba through
Bp through Bf , the only exception being Ba, which falls slightly below A on MindPower for the rea-
sons above. Abstract Stage 3: A= 73.9→B a = 75.7→Bp = 88.8→Bf = 98.4. MindPower (overall
layer-mean): A= 19.1→B a = 18.3→Bp = 24.1→Bf = 27.3. The ordering rules out non-perceptual
explanations: if scaffolds helped through token-count or format-priming, Ba would close more of the
gap and Bp would not be reliably below Bf . Excluding the automatic parser, the ordering A < Bp <
Bf holds per model for6 of 7 models on abstract Stage 3 (Table 3); the exception is Llama 4 Maverick,
whose Bp (85.0%) falls below its high visual-only baseline (87.0%). A stripped variant Bvis−stripped
removes the explicit visibility and absence statements that match nine regex patterns from Bf (§G.2;
8.9% of characters). On the six models evaluated, accuracy is 90.0% and PCRvis−stripped = 0.69,
recovering most of the gap. The one outlier is Gemini 2.5 Pro (54.4%, against 88.0% or above for
every other model), indicating model-specific reliance on explicit absence cues (Table S4).

accuracy is94.5%. It appears only when the question is conveyed visually and disappears when the
visibility-and-absence content is provided in the scaffold. The mechanism is a perceptual front-end
that encodeswhatan object is andwhereit currently is but notwho was present when it moved,
leaving the inferential pathway with insufficient state to dissociate reality from belief. Figure 4 shows

5 Discussion
Scene-state extraction is the dominant bottleneck:The findings converge on a single picture. When
the scene is given in pixels, frontier VLMs fail false-belief tasks by margins commonly attributed to a
missing belief-reasoning capacity. When the same problem is given in text, the same models succeed.
Given pixels plus the structured perceptual content the front-end should have produced, performance
reaches or exceeds the text-only ceiling on abstract panels and MuMA-ToM, and recovers part of
it on MindPower. Under the evaluated protocol, the deficit lies predominantly not in the inferential
pathway that operates on scene representations, which the text-only ceiling shows is largely intact,
but in the front-end that converts pixels to scene representations. Reality bias names the failure:
the front-end encodes object identity and current location but not who was present at each moment,
leaving the inferential pathway with the wrong input.
Reconciliation with prior accounts:Recent cognitive or representational diagnoses of visual ToM
failures [19, 8, 34, 13] target single-image or static tasks with spatially localized evidence, so they
can coexist with our claim. The protocol distinguishes the two diagnoses per task: a dose-response
with PCRp or PCRf near 1 localizes the deficit to perceptual extraction, and a flat one would
localize it to inference. For sequential multi-panel and multi-frame ToM, the signature consistently
localizes to perception, and it supplies the measurement that model-based approaches [26, 14] skip
by taking scene structure as given (§E).
Architectural implications:If the bottleneck is in front-end scene-state extraction rather than belief
inference, the first priority is better perceptual representations rather than new reasoning modules.
The specific competences current encoders fail to produce are object-level entity tracking with
persistent identity across frames, and presence-absence registers indexed to entities at each frame. We
predict that VLM architectures with explicit object slots, cross-frame correspondence, and visibility
tracking, pretrained for per-entity state extraction rather than global scene captioning, would close
most of the visual-to-text gap on existing ToM benchmarks without belief-specific training objectives.
6 Limitations
We evaluate two stimulus regimes and seven black-box API models; in-the-wild video and
open-weight object-centric models are untested, and the claims are functional, not mechanistic. The
perception/reasoning boundary follows our operational definition (§3): cross-frame event linking is
an intermediate representation, and a definition assigning it to reasoning would move that increment
accordingly. The corruption controls are model-dependent and partly weak by construction, and
Dmismatch may net useful template cues against misleading scene content, so they argue against text
volume without settling whether the benefit requires image-bound content; jointly varying image-text
alignment is a natural next test (§F).
7 Conclusion
We proposed a five-condition dissociation protocol that holds the inferential task constant while
varying perceptual fidelity. Across seven VLMs and roughly 159,000 API calls, the visual ToM
deficit is predominantly perceptual: ground-truth scaffolds substantially close the visual-to-text gap
on every regime, automatic parsers fail in aggregate, the recovery tracks cross-frame event structure,
and the dominant visual-only error is reality bias. Under the evaluated protocol, structured visual
scene-state extraction, rather than belief inference, is the dominant bottleneck for visual ToM.

A Implementation details
Reproducibility map.Every protocol element in the main text has a corresponding entry here:
stimulus generation and seed formula (§A.1); automatic parser (§A.2); API request parameters,
retry logic, and call accounting (§A.3); answer grading (§A.4); error categorization (§A.5); prompt
paraphrases (§A.6); statistical tests (§D); exact model identifiers (§F); ablation scaffolds and controls

stimulus generator, and complete logged responses are released at the URL given in the abstract.
A.1 Stimulus generation
All 1,000 stimuli are generated programmatically using PIL (Python Imaging Library). Each panel
measures 200×200 pixels. Multi-panel stimuli are created by concatenating panels horizontally,
yielding images of width 200×N panels where Npanels is the panel count (4–6 depending on template).
Adjacent panels are separated by a 2-pixel light gray (#DDDDDD) divider.
Entity rendering parameters.Characters (agents) are rendered as filled circles with radius 18 px.
Objects are smaller filled circles with radius 10 px (12 px for Stage 1 templates). Containers are filled
rectangles of 58×38 px. Obstacles and doors are gray ( #888888) or brown (#8D6E63) rectangles.
Room boundaries are drawn as 2-pixel black outlines.
Color palettes.Six agent colors: blue ( #1565C0), red ( #C62828), green ( #2E7D32), purple
(#6A1B9A), orange ( #E65100), teal ( #00838F). Five object colors: yellow ( #FFD600), amber
(#FF6F00), lime ( #76FF03), pink ( #E040FB), cyan ( #00E5FF). Six container colors: light-blue
(#42A5F5), coral ( #EF5350), brown ( #5D4037), mint ( #66BB6A), violet ( #AB47BC), peach
(#FF7043). For each probe variant, character colors, object colors, and container colors are sampled
without replacement from their respective palettes using a seeded random number generator (seed
=10,000×(template_index+1)+variant_index), ensuring full reproducibility.
Zero text constraint.No text labels, panel numbers, character names, or narration appear in any
stimulus image.
A.2 Automatic vision parser
The parser operates on the saved PNG image with no access to generation metadata.
Foreground masking.A pixel is classified as foreground if it is not white ( RGB>210 on all
channels), not black ( <50 on all channels), not gray (channel spread <30 and mean between
80–210), and not door-brown (|R−141|<25∧|G−110|<25∧|B−99|<25).

Connected component extraction.Flood-fill BFS from each unvisited foreground pixel, expanding
to 4-connected neighbors within ∆RGB <60 from the seed pixel. Components smaller than 40 pixels
are discarded. Components are capped at 6,000 pixels to prevent runaway fills.
Entity classification.Each blob is classified by area and circularity (area / bounding-box area):
•Agent: circular (0.55≤circularity≤0.90, aspect ratio0.65–1.35) and area 400–2,000 px.
•Object: circular and area 100–550 px.
•Container: non-circular and area≥1,500px.
Color identity is assigned by nearest-neighbor matching against the known palette (Euclidean
distance in RGB, threshold 80).
Event inference.Agent presence is compared across consecutive panels. If an agent present in
panel k is absent in panel k+1, an exit event is inferred. Object position (left/right of panel center)
is tracked to infer transfer events.
Scaffold serialization.Parsed structure is serialized as: “Panel k: agents: [name] agent ([side]-
[vertical]). objects: [name] object ([side]-[vertical]). containers: [name] container ([side]-[vertical]).”
A.3 API call protocol
All models are accessed via the OpenRouter API (https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/
completions).
Request parameters.Temperature: 0.0. Max tokens: 200. Single-turn conversation (one user
message). Images are included as base64-encoded PNG in the image_url field for conditions A,
Ba, Bp, Bf , and all controls. Condition C sends text only (no image).
Retry logic.HTTP 429 (rate limit): exponential backoff starting at 30 s. HTTP 5xx: retry after 15 s.
Maximum 3 retries per call. Failed calls are recorded as incorrect with anERRORresponse prefix.
Parallelization.Multiple API keys support parallel workers. Task queue distributes probe-model-
condition triples. Results are checkpointed every 100 completions.
Call accounting and cost.The paper-facing benchmark used 108,500 API calls: 60,200 for the

A.4 Answer grading
Responses are normalized (lowercased, whitespace-collapsed) before grading. Grading is
answer-type-specific:
•yes_no: Response must start with the expected token (“yes” or “no”).
•number: Expected digit must appear in the first 4 characters.
• left_right: Response must contain any of the expected aliases (e.g., “left”, “left box”, container

•belief_split(reality-vs-belief template): The response is split at the word “belief” (or at midpoint

•All other types: Response must contain at least one expected alias.
No partial credit is awarded. The grading function is deterministic and identical across all conditions

D Statistical methodology
D.1 Bootstrap confidence intervals
All confidence intervals are computed via probe-level bootstrap resampling. For each condition,
the accuracy matrix has dimensions M×N probes (models × probes). We resample Nprobes probe
indices with replacement 10,000 times, compute the mean accuracy across all models and resampled
probes for each bootstrap iteration, and report the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles as the 95% CI.

D.2 McNemar tests
McNemar’s test is applied to matched pairs: for each (model, probe_id) combination, we compare
whether the response was correct under condition X vs. condition Y , yielding a 2×2 contingency
table. The test statistic is χ2 = (b−c)2/(b+c), compared against χ21. We apply Bonferroni correction
across 12 pairwise comparisons ( αBonf = 0.05/12 = 0.0042). Stage 3 yields n= 7×500 = 3,500
paired observations per comparison. The five comparisons added for the scaffold-content ladder
and corruption controls ( Bidentity, Bstatic, Dmismatch, Dnoise, Dshuffled, each against A) use the
same uncorrected statistic on the same 3,500 matched pairs against the original Condition A, and
are evaluated against the same αBonf = 0.0042. Under a single correction over all 17 comparisons
(α= 0.05/17 = 0.0029), every conclusion holds except one: Bstatic (p=.0033 ) is significant at
αBonf = 0.0042but not at0.0029, so we treat it as a weak effect.
D.3 PCR bootstrap
The Perceptual Contribution Ratio is bootstrapped by resampling probe indices and computing
PCRx = (AccB −AccA)/(AccC −AccA)for each bootstrap sample.

Interpretation.(A1) anchors the metric to the construct: a scaffold that fails to improve over

interval-scale quantity with neither a canonical zero (chance levels differ across question formats
and answer cardinalities) nor a fixed unit (proportions vs. percentages); the perceptual-vs-conceptual
decomposition should be invariant to such reparametrizations. (A4) formalizes PCR as ashare:
equal absolute gains in scaffolded accuracy contribute equally to the recovery score, and a stochastic
mixture of two scaffolds receives the convex combination of their individual PCRs, the natural
property for a metric reporting the fraction of a gap closed.

E Extended discussion of competing accounts
Several recent works argue that VLM failures on social reasoning tasks are cognitive rather than
perceptual. We discuss each in detail and explain why our findings are complementary rather than
contradictory.
Liuet al. [ 19]: “Seeing but Not Believing.”This work demonstrates that VLMs internally attend
to correct visual evidence yet still output incorrect answers on VQA benchmarks. The key distinction
from our work lies in theperceptual extraction complexityof the tasks. Their benchmarks involve
single-image VQA where the relevant visual evidence occupies a spatially localized region within
one frame. Our stimuli requiresequential multi-panel entity tracking. Our data directly address
whether this distinction holds: the 60.0% reality-bias error rate shows that models correctly identify
where the object currently is but fail to track who was absent when it moved.
Chenet al. [ 8]: “VLMs See What You Want but Not What You See.”VLMs achieve high
performance on intent recognition but consistently fail at perspective-taking, scoringbelow chance
on PerspectBench. Our work offers a complementary interpretation: the egocentric bias they observe
is precisely what our reality-bias error category captures (549 of 915 visual-only Stage 3 errors;
60.0%). We demonstrate that this bias disappears when perceptual scaffolding provides explicit
agent-perspective information ( Bf : 98.4% vs. A: 73.9%).
Tonget al. [ 34]: “Eyes Wide Shut.”Their identification of systematic visual shortcomings in
multimodal LLMs traceable to CLIP-based encoders provides complementary evidence from the
representation-learning side. Our scaffolding intervention demonstrates the downstream consequence:
when structured perceptual content is provided externally, the reasoning succeeds, consistent with
their finding that visual grounding remains an open challenge.
Zhanget al. [ 39]: “MindPower.”Their BDI framework decomposes ToM reasoning into
perception, mental reasoning, decision, and action layers, finding that VLMs perform poorly across
all layers but especially on mental reasoning. Our work is consistent with their per-layer analysis
and adds thescaffolding interventionthat isolates the perceptual from the conceptual component.
Jinet al. [ 14]: “MMToM-QA.”Our benchmark differs in three key respects: (i) Abstract 2D
panels rather than naturalistic 3D game scenes; (ii) a scaffolding intervention that disentangles
perception from reasoning; (iii) evaluation across a developmental hierarchy rather than testing false
belief in isolation.
Shapiraet al. [ 28]: “Clever Hans or Neural Theory of Mind?”Our work operates in the
visual modality and reaches a structurally different conclusion: for frontier models, the inferential
competence (demonstrated by 94.5% text-only accuracy) is available in the text modality, and
the visual failure is attributable to perceptual extraction, not pattern matching.
Huet al. [ 13]: “Re-evaluating Theory of Mind Evaluation in LLMs.”Our five-condition
protocol directly addresses this concern: by holding the reasoning task constant and varying only
the perceptual input modality, we isolate whether the model is performing genuine belief-reality
dissociation or relying on linguistic shortcuts.
F Additional limitations and caveats
Stimulus and model generalization.Our evaluation covers two stimulus regimes, abstract 2D pan-
els and naturalistic 3D game scenes from MuMA-ToM and MindPower. Generalization to in-the-wild
human video, surveillance footage, or first-person egocentric video is not established and is a natural
next test. The model lineup is limited to seven systems available through a commercial API during
March 2026 (main protocol) and July 2026 (§G.6, §G.7); ToM-specific fine-tunes, open-weight
models with object-centric objectives, and not-yet-deployed multimodal systems are not represented.
Methodological scope.Models are accessed as black boxes through a single inference provider
with deterministic decoding; we measure functional consequences on outputs, not attention patterns

or internal representations. The MindPower Bf scaffold is an oracle full-information condition rather
than a leakage-controlled perceptual ceiling; the perceptual-only finding for MindPower rests on Bp,
and the MindPower PCRp values (Table 2c) are the appropriate quantitative summary on that regime.
Reality-bias categorization is based on a deterministic algorithm (§A.5) and inherits its definitions.
Answer balance.Allsally_anne_search(50 probes) andsally_anne_belief(50 probes) have
expected answer “left.” Allsally_anne_reality(50 probes) have expected answer “right.” Because
every template contributes exactly 50 probes, template-reweighted PCR (§G.10) coincides with the
probe-level PCR.
Abstract 2D panels.The Heider-and-Simmel-style abstraction deliberately isolates cognitive
capacity from appearance-based recognition. The perceptual bottleneck identified here is a lower
bound: naturalistic 3D game scenes impose greater perceptual demands. Generalization from
Abstract 2D panels to naturalistic 3D game scenes is tested separately by MuMA-ToM and
MindPower rather than inferred from the panel experiments.
Perception/reasoning boundary.The attribution of the deficit to perception is made relative to the
operational definition in §3: structured scene-state extraction, including cross-frame correspondence
and event detection. Cross-frame event linking is an intermediate representation that belief inference
consumes, and it can itself be viewed as reasoning-relevant. The scaffold-content ladder (§G.6)
shows that the recovery is driven by this event structure rather than by entity names or static positions;
a definition that assigns event linking to reasoning would move that increment to the reasoning side,
while leaving the finding that belief-level inference from access facts is not the limiting step.
Interpretation of the mismatch control.Because Dmismatch partners share the template, they carry
useful template-level information while misdescribing the specific scene. Its small net effect may
therefore reflect two opposing effects rather than the absence of any effect. The corruption effects
are also strongly model-dependent, and Dnoise and Dshuffled are weak by construction. We therefore
treat the controls as evidence against a text-volume account, not as proof that the benefit requires
image-bound content. Designing multimodal augmentations that jointly manipulate the visual and
textual inputs while systematically varying their alignment is a promising direction for future work.
Broader impacts.This work is diagnostic: it trains no models and uses procedurally generated
stimuli and existing public benchmarks, with no human subjects or personal data. On the positive
side, localizing visual ToM failures to perceptual extraction can direct effort toward front-ends that
make assistive, educational, and collaborative systems more reliable at tracking what the people
they serve have and have not seen. On the negative side, better tracking of who saw what could
also strengthen systems used for surveillance or for manipulation that exploits gaps in a person’s
knowledge. Our protocol measures this capability rather than providing it, and we release stimuli
and evaluation code only, not models.

G.4 Experiment 4: Chain-of-thought self-scaffolding
Each model in this ablation receives a system prompt instructing step-by-step panel description,

G.5 Experiment 5: Self-scaffold generate-then-reason
Each model in this ablation first generates a panel-by-panel scene description (max 600 tokens,

then-reason format, and the other affected models lose accuracy. The result is consistent with
the Bvlm finding (§G.1): models’ own visual descriptions contain too many entity-identity and
event-detection errors to serve as effective scaffolds for downstream belief reasoning.

G.7 Experiment 7: Length-matched corrupted scaffolds
Three corruptions of the correct perceptual scaffold Bp test whether gains require correct content
rather than scaffold format or text volume. Each keeps the format of Bp and is evaluated on the
same 500 Stage 3 probes and seven models.
•D mismatch: the Bp scaffold of a different variant of the same template, with whitespace-token
length matched exactly. Within each template, variants are sorted and each is paired with the next,
wrapping around, so all 500 partners share their target’s expected answer.
•D noise: the correct Bp scaffold in which each entity name is independently replaced, with
probability 0.3, by a different name from its category. This is not a one-to-one permutation;
collisions and cascading substitutions can occur, and most names remain correct.
•D shuffled: the correct Bp scaffold with panel sentences reordered. The “Panel k” prefixes are
retained, so the original order is recoverable; we treat it as the weakest corruption.
On aggregate, all three corruptions recover less than half of the 14.9 pp gain that the correct scaffold
adds over A, while Bp itself differs from A at p≈4×10 −63 (McNemar, 3,500 matched pairs, §D.2).
Dmismatch gives no significant gain (+0.8 pp; b= 520, c= 549, p=.38 ). Dnoise (+5.6 pp; b= 422,
c= 618, p= 1.2×10 −9) and Dshuffled (+6.7 pp; b= 413, c= 648, p= 5.4×10 −13) give small but
significant gains, well below the +14.9 pp of Bp (b= 226, c= 750). These gains come mainly from
Ministral-8B (+17.4 to +33.0 pp over its A); on the other six models the effects are −1.9, +2.2,
and+2.3pp (A= 77.2%).
The per-model pattern is heterogeneous. Gemini 2.5 Pro falls well below A under every corruption
(−14.2 to −26.8 pp) and Claude Sonnet 4 retains little of its Bp gain, whereas GPT-4o retains nearly
all of it (+11.0 to +13.2 of +13.8 pp), and GPT-4.1-mini and Ministral-8B retain most of it under
Dnoise and Dshuffled. Because every Dmismatch partner shares the template and expected answer,
it preserves the template’s event structure while misdescribing the scene; the models that retain the
gain under it appear to read that event structure from the text. The controls thus argue against text
volume as the source of the gains, but they do not show that the benefit requires content bound to
the specific image (§F).
G.8 Scaffold length
Scaffold length does not track accuracy. The zero-shot VLM-parser scaffolds (§G.1) are the longest
we use, with a mean of 343 whitespace tokens, yet give no improvement over A ( PCR =−0.04
and −0.27 for the two parsers). The structured scaffolds span 107 to 136 tokens, and the text-only
prompt C, which yields the strongest text-side accuracy, is the shortest at about 40 tokens. Finally,
image-plus-scaffold exceeds text-only ( Bf 98.4% vs. C 94.5%, PCRf >1 ), but because the Bf
scaffold and the C narration differ in their text as well as in the image, this comparison does not
by itself isolate the contribution of the image. On MindPower, where C is exactly the Bp description
without the storyboard, adding the storyboard lowers accuracy (Bp 24.1%vs. C33.0%).

H Abstract 2D panels dataset overview
The Abstract 2D panels benchmark is designed to evaluate whether VLM failures on visual
false-belief tasks are better explained by perceptual bottlenecks or by reasoning bottlenecks. It spans
object tracking, goal attribution, and Theory of Mind cases, and it is intentionally organized to mirror
the developmental cascade described in the paper: object individuation at 3–5 months, goal attribution
at 6–12 months, and false-belief reasoning at roughly 4 years. This developmental structure is part
of the benchmark’s justification: later false-belief tasks depend on the representational outputs of
earlier perceptual achievements, so failures on visual ToM probes may reflect missing perceptual
state extraction rather than missing belief reasoning. The benchmark is also intentionally designed
to keep the answer space short and tightly controlled so that errors are less confounded by free-form
generation. Most targets are therefore simple outputs such as yes/no, left/right, a number, or
a short phrase, with only two genuinely multi-token templates: unexpected_contents (round
objects) and reality_vs_belief (Actually: right. Belief: left. ). The examples
below focus on representative cases that make the contrast between image-based and text-only
conditions especially easy to inspect.
